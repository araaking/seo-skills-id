## 1. Encyclopedia pages (9.1, p. 85)

- Some encyclopedias are standard references; others are written anonymously with no editorial oversight or fact-checking. Because individual authors are often unknown, E-E-A-T is judged through the **site's reputation**.
- High/Highest only for encyclopedias with a very good reputation for accuracy and expertise, **and** well-researched, sourced articles.
- **Wikipedia:** generally well regarded and popular, but no single author or organization vouches for accuracy, and quality varies. Judge each article:
  - Non-YMYL topic, accurate and satisfying info, trustworthy external references → usually **High** range.
  - Thinner MC and references → often **Medium**.
  - Very little MC → lower.
  - **Factual errors** → enough for Low or Lowest.

## 2. Error pages and pages with no MC (9.2, pp. 85–86)

Key question: **did the site owner try to help visitors** who land there?

- **Explicit error pages / custom 404s** → often **Medium**: they explain the problem and help navigation.
- Custom 404 with high effort and original content that's truly satisfying → can be **High**, occasionally **Highest**.
- **"Broken" pages** → often **Low**: they fail their purpose, even when the fault is only temporary.
- **Systemically broken** → **Lowest**. To tell the difference, check other pages on the same site. If many share the problem, it reflects systemic lack of effort.

| Example | Rating | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| A page with no MC, only ads; hundreds of other pages on the site look the same | Lowest | Deliberately no MC, systemic |
| A reputable local newspaper page that didn't load and showed no error; navigation still worked; later fixed | Low | An error that slipped through before going live (lack of effort), temporary |
| Custom 404 on a big reputable store, only a minimal explanation and a homepage link | Medium | Nothing wrong, nothing special |
| Custom 404 that explains the issue well and gives tips, including a search box | High | Satisfying MC, positive site reputation |
| Custom 404 from a publication famous for its cartoons: a cartoon, a witty caption and site-specific search | High+ to Highest | Clear effort and talent, very original, very positive reputation |

## 3. Forums and Q&A (9.3, pp. 87–90)

**Rating principles:**
- **MC = the question + answers + resulting discussion.**
- Rate from the viewpoint of a **visitor** arriving from search, not a participant.
- Usernames/aliases alone don't prevent a High or Highest rating.
- Discussion E-E-A-T can often be judged from the posts and comments themselves. On some topics, Experience matters most for Trust; for others, Expertise. Sometimes posters mention their own experience/expertise, or others comment on it.
- YMYL topics need extra attention to Trust.

**Quality spectrum:**
- **Highest:** very satisfying conversation; participants put real effort into posts and have rich experience/expertise; depth, unique insight, or experiences hard to find in one's own surroundings.
- **Low:** little effort (few, shallow replies), lacking experience/expertise, mild inaccuracies, or disrespect that would put others off joining.
- **Lowest:** harmfully misleading info/advice, contradicts expert consensus, or encourages harm to self/others.

**Tricky cases:**
- **A new question with no answers yet** → generally **Medium** unless other Low traits exist. It needs time to achieve its purpose.
- **Long without participation** or meaningful answers → can be **Low**, especially with other Low traits.
- **Discussion that drifts, gets heated, or fills with spam/misleading content:** value the meaningful discussion that remains. Mixed high and low quality with meaningful discussion → **Medium** may fit best, as long as it isn't potentially harmful.

**Examples (condensed from pp. 88–90):**

| Page | Rating | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Q&A on chest pain and smoking (YMYL) | Lowest | Hard-to-understand question; few replies; one suggests switching to marijuana, one says keep smoking and take aspirin, another insults the asker. Wrong and dangerous medical advice |
| Q&A on a part for an old car | Low | The "answer" is useless (treat as unanswered); a "sponsored answer" formatted like a real answer is actually an ad = misleading design |
| A new, in-depth forum question only hours old | Medium | No chance for discussion yet, but no Low traits |
| Forum on an auction site about vacuum cleaners | Medium (mixed) | Many participants share detailed experience (e.g., how well they handle pet hair), but there's distracting content. **Not a beauty contest**: a cluttered page can still be valuable |
| Forum on washing ballet shoes | Medium (mixed) | A community of ballet dancers sharing first-hand experience, but relatively little participation and depth; balanced by the forum's topic-specific E-E-A-T |
| Forum post on the magic-loop knitting technique | High | The poster shares a guide and photos of her own work; 20+ years knitting socks, reasonably considered an expert |
| Q&A on whether a robot vacuum suits a particular need | High | Many detailed owner experiences; satisfying MC |
| Designer handbag authentication forum | High | Go-to forum for luxury bags; expert members can tell real from fake; ads present but don't distract from the MC |
| Aquarium/paludarium landscaping forum | High | Lots of interaction on materials and species; niche expertise |
| Q&A on abbreviations in writing | Highest | Reference site for writing/publishing with a very good reputation and authority |
| Q&A "how long do most cancer patients live" (YMYL) | Highest | Answered by non-experts with lived experience (how long loved ones lived after diagnosis). The focus is experience, not medical advice, which is very helpful for this topic |

## 4. Practical PQ task rules (10.0–10.1, p. 91)

- **Don't agonize over one rating.** Torn between two → choose the **lower**. Torn between three → choose the **middle**.
- **A site's country doesn't affect PQ.** US English raters use the same standards for English-language sites from the UK or Canada.
- If the landing page isn't a normal web page (PDF, image file), some criteria may not apply; use judgment.
- The guidelines don't cover every aspect of quality. If a rater is convinced a page is High or Low for a reason not covered, they may rate it so and explain.
- A PQ task has initial questions, a "PQ grid" (observation notes), an overall rating slider and a comment box. Tasks stop early for pages that are porn, in a foreign language, fail to load, or have restricted/inaccessible MC (e.g., subscription required).
- The **Foreign Language** flag isn't used if the language is the task language, a language widely spoken by people in the task location, or English. **Did Not Load** only applies when there is no site-created content at all (no MC, SC or ads).

## 5. Page Quality FAQ (11.0, pp. 92–93)

- **Why so many steps?** For a well-considered judgment. Pages that first look Low may turn out Medium/High on inspection, and vice versa.
- **Do pages that "look good" get High automatically?** No. The steps exist precisely to avoid shallow, appearance-based judgments.
- **Is sharing experience/opinion a beneficial purpose, even if the opinion offends?** Yes; seeing others' perspectives has value. Apply the guideline standards, not personal views. But opinions can spread misleading info or cause harm, so standards still apply. **Harm assessment comes first and overrides other considerations**, including the creator's experience or expertise.
- **Can lived experience make a YMYL page Highest?** Factual info and advice on YMYL should come from experts. Sharing lived experience on YMYL can be Highest if it's trustworthy, safe, consistent with expert consensus, and on a topic where experience is valuable. Example: an inspiring post about voting for the first time after becoming a citizen can be Highest; but if it gives wrong info on when and where to vote, deliberately or carelessly → **Lowest**, since it could cost people their vote.
- **Does expertise matter for every topic?** Informal expertise matters as much as formal, and is more common for some topics. Niche or non-mainstream fields have experts too. Example: there are alternative-medicine sites by leading practitioners (acupuncture, herbalism) and others by people lacking both expertise and experience; E-E-A-T should tell them apart. **If a page is harmful, untrustworthy or spammy, expertise and experience are irrelevant: still Lowest.**
- **What's the difference between Experience and Expertise?** Expertise tends to be objective and testable (e.g., calculating a bridge's load). Experience is more subjective and often shared through personal narrative (e.g., what falling in love feels like). In practice there's no need to separate them rigidly; focus on what content is trustworthy and satisfying for the page's purpose. They can complement each other: a medical site for treatment options, other patients' experiences for emotional support and a sense of what to expect.
- **Are art pages with no other purpose automatically Low?** No. Artistic expression, humor, entertainment, sharing photos/videos are all valid purposes.
- **What about interstitials?** Ignore them if the MC is easy to reach. If an interstitial makes the MC very hard or impossible to reach, it factors into the rating.
- **What if no High page of a certain type has ever existed?** Standards must be uniform for all pages, including ones not yet created. There may be none now but there could be later.
- **Is celebrity gossip always Low?** No. There's high- and low-quality gossip. High if accurate, interesting, free of Low/Lowest traits, and from a reasonably reliable source. Celebrity stories can inspire or help people facing similar challenges.

## 6. Implications for content creators

**[Application]**
- **A custom 404 is a small investment that pays off:** explain the problem, provide search and links to popular pages. Make sure errors aren't systemic (audit broken pages regularly).
- **Forums/communities/comment sections:** moderate to keep civility, remove spam, and encourage experience-based answers. Mark expert-verified answers on YMYL topics.
- **Self-made Q&A pages** (FAQ, "ask an expert"): answers must be substantive. Throwaway answers or disguised "sponsored answers" signal Low.
- **Experience content on YMYL topics** is very valuable when it honestly positions itself as experience rather than professional advice and contains no wrong facts.
- **When unsure about your own content's rating, pick the lower one** (same rule as raters), then improve until confident.
