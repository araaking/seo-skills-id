## 1. The five Needs Met levels (13.0, p. 113)

Needs Met focuses on **user needs**: how helpful and satisfying a result is for the query.

| Rating | Short definition |
|---|---|
| **Fully Meets (FullyM)** | Special category: only for queries clearly seeking **one specific result**, when the result is exactly that |
| **Highly Meets (HM)** | Very helpful for the dominant or a common interpretation, or a reasonable minor one |
| **Moderately Meets (MM)** | Helpful for the dominant or a common interpretation, or a reasonable minor one |
| **Slightly Meets (SM)** | Less helpful for a reasonable interpretation, **or** helpful for an unlikely minor interpretation |
| **Fails to Meet (FailsM)** | Completely fails all or nearly all users (off-topic, or serves a "no chance" interpretation) |

The slider has in-between positions (e.g., HM+), like the PQ slider.

## 2. What to rate: block content vs landing page (13.1, pp. 114–117)

Each result = result block content + its landing page.

**Standard rule:**
- **Special Content Result Block (SCRB):** block content always plays a large role. If it links to a landing page, ask: would users need to click?
  - Most wouldn't → rate **the block content alone**.
  - Some/many would → you may consider the landing page too; **both must help** for a high rating.
  - Assume interactive features work; try interacting since some links/buttons may be live.
- **Web Search Result Block:** a click is needed; **rate the landing page**.

**Example (p. 115):** for a query about the meaning of love, an SCRB already full of helpful content with no obvious landing-page link is rated on its content. A regular web result must be clicked to get an answer, so it's rated on its landing page.

**SCRBs rated mainly on block content (p. 116):** info on a local animal hospital, a city's weather, calories in a banana, how to find a credit card's security code, how a US president died, movie showtimes in a city.

**Note:** if a PQ rating is requested for an SCRB with a landing page, PQ is **always** based on the landing page.

**Special ratings (13.1.1, p. 117)** when a task asks for them:
- **Landing Page Needs Met:** landing page only; block content ignored (for SCRBs and web results alike).
- **Block Content Needs Met:** block content only, without clicking. For web results: just the title, snippet and any visuals in the block. For SCRBs: just the content inside the block.

## 3. Fully Meets (13.2, pp. 118–122)

**Only applies when all are true:**
1. The query interpretation and intent are **specific, clear and unambiguous**.
2. There is one site, page, Know Simple fact or similar single target that **all or nearly all** users in that locale/location are after.
3. **All or nearly all** users would be **fully satisfied** with that result.

- **Most queries can't have a Fully Meets result.** Most don't point to one site/page or one fact; different people want different results; one person often needs many results.
- For specific-fact queries: the result must give the info thoroughly, accurately and clearly from a trustworthy source. **You must check accuracy** and expert consensus before giving Fully Meets.
- **Be conservative.** When in doubt, go lower. If it comes close but the block by itself might fall short → HM or HM+.

**Fully Meets examples:**
- Website queries: a giant marketplace's name → its site; "[retailer] website" → that retailer's site; a film title + a film database name → that film's page on that database; a misspelled URL for a streaming service → that service's site; a news portal name + "health" → that portal's health section; a news portal's name → its site; a basketball player's stats + a stats site name → the player's page on that site.
- A local restaurant's phone number → a block showing the **correct** number with tap-to-call (verified against the restaurant's official site).
- Know Simple: how to find a computer's OS version; a city's population in a given year; today's forecast at the user's location; the country a mountain is in; a person's height. The block shows the complete, correct answer prominently and readably.
- Specific Visit-in-Person: a branded gas station at a specific intersection → a block for that location with visit info.
- A famous address far from the user → a map block with the address, a directions link and the landmark's name. Given a **range** because some users want more (photos, description). For such a specific query, distance from the user doesn't change the rating.

**Queries that can't be Fully Meets (13.2.2):**
| Type | Example | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Broad query | [knitting] | People want videos, instructions, patterns, etc.; no single official source |
| Famous names | A former president's name | Broad info needs, impossible to know exactly what's wanted |
| Ambiguous query with no dominant interpretation | [ada] | Could be a disability law, a dental association or a diabetes association; each has an official homepage but none is dominant |
| Non-famous people's names | An ordinary person's name | Many people share it; unclear who's being sought |

## 4. Highly Meets (13.3, pp. 123–129)

**Definition:** very helpful and highly satisfying for the dominant or a common interpretation, or a reasonable minor one.

**Common traits (depending on the query):**
- Entertaining, for queries with a reasonable entertainment intent.
- Reflects **what real people think, see and have lived through**, where no single right answer exists.
- For informational queries: **easy to understand** (helpful images/video, digestible summary) **or deep/insightful** (thorough explanation, expert analysis).
- For queries needing new content: **fresh**, covering what's being talked about right now (breaking news, the latest viral video, a new fashion trend).

**Key principles:**
- Any format: articles, short/long video, images, social posts, forum discussions.
- **No length requirement.** Sometimes what satisfies is short and easy; sometimes it's depth. For truly complex or YMYL topics, completeness can be critical because a short answer could mislead.
- **Accuracy is required** for informational HM results. YMYL must be accurate and trustworthy. Medical/scientific info must follow consensus, **unless the user clearly seeks an alternative view**.
- A query can have many HM results. Search engines should show **diversity**: different sites, content types, people and perspectives, especially for questions without one right answer, subjective queries, multi-intent queries, opinion-seeking, or exploration.
- **For most queries, HM is the highest rating possible.** The bar is very high. Some queries have no HM result at all, either because such content hasn't been made or because it can't be found.

**Highly Meets examples:**
- A supermarket chain's name + user location → a map block with **all** branches in that city plus visit info. Not Fully Meets because people wanting the site (coupons, promos) need other results. Conversely, the official site is also HM, not FullyM, because those wanting to visit must dig for location info.
- A famous museum's name, user in the same city → the official site (exhibitions, membership, shop) = HM; an info block about the museum = HM, especially for visitors. An info block with a site link isn't as satisfying as a full web result (title, snippet, URL) for those wanting the site.
- A library's name, in a town with only one library by that name → its info block = HM.
- A famous singer's name → their rich official site (news, tour dates, music, photos, video). Intent is too broad for Fully Meets.
- Reviews of a restaurant → a landing page with hundreds of reviews.
- Coffee shops nearby → a block listing popular shops around the user's location dot, clickable for calls, directions and reviews. Not FullyM given the sheer number of coffee shops.
- The country a mountain is in → a landing page that answers plus lots of info about the mountain (answer not shown prominently in the block).
- Theater tickets in New York → a landing page with many ticket options.
- Kids' backpacks → a well-known maker's landing page with lots of product info, reviews and filters.
- A poisonous rash plant's name → a landing page with lots of helpful info (photos, treatment).
- A legendary athlete's name → an encyclopedia article on him.
- Tutorial for a specific soccer trick → a video tutorial with narrated instructions and tips.
- A particular bridge type → **images** of that bridge, since for a distinctive design a picture helps more than text.
- Invasive species → a block with a good summary definition, though examples and impacts would add value.
- [verbena] in Austin → info on the Verbena restaurant for those wanting to visit (the likeliest interpretation there).
- An actress's name → a very comprehensive fan site (tens of thousands of photos, hundreds of clips, interviews).
- Another actress's name → a block of her popular films (a reasonable filmography intent).
- The 1969 moon landing → the page on the official site of the space agency that ran the mission, with a summary, footage and photos.
- Yet another actress → timely, interesting recent news.
- A rumor about death from mint candy + soda → a well-known fact-check site page rating the claim false and explaining why, plus the history of similar urban legends.
- Reviews of a film → a discussion post collecting review snippets from various sources plus participants' comments.
- A city name → a map block that expands into a detailed map.

## 5. Moderately Meets (13.4, pp. 130–131)

**Definition:** helpful for a reasonable interpretation/intent, including reasonable minor ones. Fewer valuable attributes than HM; still "fits", but less satisfying.

- It may: represent fewer real-people opinions/experiences; be a bit harder to understand; be slightly less current for queries needing new content.
- MM fits when some aspects look HM and others look SM.
- **Nothing is "wrong" with MM.** Not outdated or inaccurate; generally average to good.

**Examples:**
- A penguin species name → a cute 14-second video of a chick walking to its parent. Helpful for those wanting cute photos/videos, but not very satisfying for the query.
- Where a US state is → a result naming bordering states, but with no visual like a map.
- An actress's name → her official site, but thin and not updated for years.
- How much a camel costs, user in Las Vegas → an overview that's a useful starting point but doesn't address what a user in that location likely wants (local purchase price or upkeep costs).

## 6. Slightly Meets (13.5, pp. 132–134)

**Definition:** less helpful for a reasonable interpretation, **or** helpful for an unlikely minor interpretation.

- Can also fit results that touch the need without fully meeting it, **provided they offer some value**.
- **But:** if the user seeks specific info and the result doesn't give it, or **misses a key aspect of the query** → not SM but **Fails to Meet**.
- Can be held back by outdated info.
- Often nothing is "wrong"; it's just a weaker fit for this query. An SM result for one query can be HM for another.
- **A very misleading or exaggerated title → SM or lower**, because of the bad experience when the landing page doesn't match what the SERP title promised. (SERP titles usually come from the pages.)

**Examples:**
- A famous singer's name → a decade-plus-old article about her divorce filing (stale news).
- A car model's name → thorough info from a reputable source, but about a model over 10 years old (stale; users are assumed to want the current model).
- [hot dog] → a page about an obscure 1984 film of the same name (unlikely minor interpretation).
- Which sharks live in rivers → a block naming only one species.
- A tech company's name → a block of its logo images (unlikely minor intent).
- A major bank's name, US user → a definition of the common word that shares the bank's name (very few want it).
- Bears swimming → a page selling a T-shirt of a bear swimming in a human sports race.
- Allergy symptoms → a news article on falling leaves as an allergy trigger. It lists some seasonal symptoms but isn't a medical source and skips other allergies like food. For a possibly YMYL topic, a medical source would help more.
- A tennis player's fastest serve speed → the result gives related info and a partial approximate figure, not the specific fact.
- Which county a town is in → a trustworthy, accurate page where the answer is only implied by other sentences, so the reader must infer it.

## 7. Fails to Meet (13.6, pp. 135–141)

**Definition:** fails all or nearly all users. E.g., ignores a critical aspect of the query or user location; wrong or very outdated info; only serves a "no chance" interpretation; off-topic for every interpretation.

**Search results shouldn't surprise people with unpleasant content.** If users clearly aren't looking for it, these = **FailsM**: harmful to self/others, harmful to groups, harmfully misleading, untrustworthy, spam, and porn.

**Examples:**
- Can penguins climb → a page about a climbing route on a rock formation named "The Penguins" in a national park (off-topic).
- A very broad common first name → a block of two local businesses whose names include it (very unlikely to be what's wanted).
- A flight code → a block listing a church, an event venue and the nearest airport, with no flight info at all.
- German cars → the homepage of a Japanese carmaker.
- A service to remove wildlife from an attic, US user → a pest-control company in Australia (locale mismatch).
- A team's starting quarterback in a given year → a trustworthy site whose page lacks that info.
- Doctor salaries → a block answering about education costs, not salary (misleading).
- Who invented stairs → a block with a wrong answer (naming an inventor and a year, though stairs existed long before).
- A superhero's name, US user → info on a Turkish city with the same name (nearly "no chance").
- Comparing non-dairy options at two ice cream shops → an answer naming one option at one shop, failing the comparison intent.
- A film's runtime → a landing page whose "answer" is an actor's uncertain quote about the length; the context isn't convincing.
- Weather in Paris, Texas, user there → weather for Paris, France.
- Go-karts for sale → a block of go-kart arcades (for riding, not buying).
- A supermarket chain's name → branches in another city around 100 km away (too far).
- A giant marketplace's name → visit info for its corporate HQ (not even open to the public).
- The nearest large city → a news result (no help at all).
- A tennis tournament's result for a given year → a page about a different year (ignores the key aspect: the year).
- A five-year-old losing teeth → a page about pike fish teeth that happens to contain the phrase "five years old" (**keyword match only**).
- Translating a word into Spanish → a block with a completely wrong translation.
- A credit card company's name → a humorous blog that merely mentions the company.
- How to quit smoking → a landing page of meaningless text.
- An airline's customer service number → a block with the wrong number (verified on the airline's official site).
- A singer's name → a news-styled article by an unknown author falsely claiming she died, left uncorrected after being disproven. **Looking like a news site doesn't guarantee accuracy.**
- A question about a dictator's daughter → a baseless conspiracy blog. The topic matches but the content is false and misleading → FailsM. A better result would confirm he had no children, or cover a novel with a similar title.

## 8. Implications for content creators

**[Application]**
- **Aim for HM.** For most queries it's the highest rating possible. FullyM is only realistic for navigational queries to your own site or specific facts.
- **Address the query's key aspects explicitly:** year, location, quantity, comparison, "without/non", target reader. Missing one key aspect can mean FailsM, not just SM.
- **Answers must be direct and clear.** An answer that's only implied or must be inferred drops to SM. A hesitant quote doesn't count as a convincing answer.
- **Format follows need:** images for visual things, video for movement tutorials, maps for locations, tables for comparisons.
- **Freshness:** content on products/models, famous people and news must be updated; stale content drops to SM.
- **For queries with no single right answer,** present real people's experiences and perspectives (reviews, testimonials, discussion). That's an HM trait.
- **Honest titles.** Clickbait titles on the SERP lower Needs Met because the page doesn't match expectations.
- **Local:** local business pages need accurate info (phone, address, hours). Wrong contact info = FailsM.
- **Simple facts must be right.** One wrong number or phone number turns a good-looking result into FailsM.
