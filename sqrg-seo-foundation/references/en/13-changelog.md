# 13 · SQRG Changelog and Related Context

> Part 1 is condensed from Appendix 2 (p. 182) of SQRG Sep 11, 2025. Part 2 comes from sources outside SQRG, with sources noted.
> Indonesian counterpart: `references/id/13-changelog.md`.

## 1. Change history per the PDF (last two years)

| Edition | Summary of changes |
|---|---|
| **September 2025** (the edition this skill uses) | YMYL definitions updated; additional clarifying examples; minor fixes (typos, etc.) |
| **January 2025** | Lowest and Low sections aligned with Google Search Web Spam Policies, with illustrative examples (this is where expired domain abuse, site reputation abuse, scaled content abuse and the generative AI definition came in); expanded guidance on minor interpretations and intents for Needs Met; other minor changes |
| **February 2024** | Updated traits of untrustworthy pages, plus examples |
| **November 2023** | Needs Met scale definitions made simpler, with new guidance on special rating types; modernized examples (popular platforms, video formats); expanded forum guidance |

Note: media reports dated the February 2024 update to early March 2024; the PDF records it as February 2024.

## 2. Context outside SQRG (re-check before treating as fact)

- **Version status (checked October 2026):** per a ppc.land report (October 2026), the edition Google hosts is still dated September 11, 2025. Claims of a "June 2026 revision" circulating on some marketing blogs aren't reflected in the published document.
- **Spam policies and Google's AI features:** the same report says that on May 15, 2026 Google stated its spam policies, including scaled content abuse, also apply to content surfacing in AI Overviews and AI Mode. This is a Google policy statement, not part of SQRG.
- **Spam policies page (checked October 2026):** Google's "Spam policies for Google web search" page was last updated Aug 28, 2026. It now defines spam as including attempts to manipulate generative AI responses in Search, and the site reputation policy was changed for the EEA (pages may be categorized separately from the main domain instead of getting a manual action; previous EEA manual actions under this policy lifted). Summarized in `14-spam-policies.md`.
- **Update frequency:** Google usually updates these guidelines roughly once a year, sometimes more often.
- **Relationship to ranking:** Google consistently says rater ratings don't directly affect rankings; they're used to evaluate search systems.

If a user asks about the latest edition and web search is available, search first.

## 3. How to update the skill when a new edition comes out

1. Download the latest PDF from Google's official site and read its changelog (Appendix 2) to see which sections changed.
2. Compare the old and new tables of contents; note sections whose numbers or pages shifted.
3. Update only the affected reference files (see the map in `00-index.md`), including page numbers.
4. Update the edition date in `00-index.md`, `SKILL.md` and part 1 of this file.
5. Update **both language versions** (`references/en/` and `references/id/`) at the same file and section numbers so they stay equivalent.
6. If an important new concept appears, add it to `11-core-summary.md` and `12-audit-checklist.md` as well.
7. `14-spam-policies.md` follows its own source: re-check the Search Central spam policies page's "last updated" date, and update the date and changed sections in both language versions of `14`.
