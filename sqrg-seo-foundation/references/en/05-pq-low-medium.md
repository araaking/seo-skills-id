## 1. Low: definition and triggers (5.0, p. 59)

**Low** = pages that don't achieve their purpose well because they're weak in a key area or have something problematic about them. Similar to Lowest but milder.

Initial considerations: beneficial or harmless purpose; zero or mild potential for harm (not meeting Lowest); any topic or site type can be Low, with extra scrutiny for online stores, medical sites and coverage of major civic issues.

**Any one of these is enough for Low:**

| Aspect | Low trigger |
|---|---|
| MC quality | Made without enough effort, originality, talent or skill to satisfy the purpose |
| Title | Somewhat misleading, shocking or exaggerated |
| Ads and SC | Ads/SC heavily distract from or interrupt use of the MC |
| Site/creator info | Unsatisfying for the page's purpose (for personal content on social/forums, an alias is enough) |
| Reputation | Mildly negative reputation of site/creator |
| E-E-A-T | Not adequate for the page's purpose |

Low pages can appear on any site, including academic, nonprofit and government sites. YMYL needs closer checking. **A single Low attribute is enough.**

## 2. Low due to insufficient E-E-A-T (5.1, p. 60)

Examples:
- **Lacking experience:** a restaurant review by someone who never ate there.
- **Lacking expertise:** a skydiving how-to by someone with no expertise.
- **Not an authoritative/trustworthy source for the topic:** tax forms offered on a recipe site.
- **Not trustworthy for its purpose:** a shopping page with minimal customer service info.

**Important:** insufficient E-E-A-T for the page's purpose can't be offset by positive reputation or site type. Good sites can have Low pages.

## 3. Low due to MC quality (5.2, pp. 60–62)

**Principle:** MC made without enough effort/originality/talent/skill. Mild inaccuracies on informational pages = evidence of Low. If the inaccuracies are harmful or so numerous the page feels untrustworthy → Lowest.

**Low MC examples (condensed from the table on p. 60):**

| Page | Why Low |
|---|---|
| A basic craft tutorial with lots of useless padding at the top (common facts about materials, off-topic info) | Not enough effort to present the instructions clearly |
| A "best vacuum cleaners" list with images and reviews paraphrased/summarized from other sources | Little effort and no first-hand experience |
| A slideshow of 10 world destinations built from others' photos and existing lists | Little effort and originality |
| A "how many cm in a meter" page so full of off-topic content the answer is hard to find | Little effort, excessive filler, shallow, only common facts |
| A list of the world's tallest mountains that omits Everest | Inaccurate and low effort |

**The title is MC too.** It represents the page in search results. Misleading, exaggerated or shocking titles = evidence of Low because of the bad experience when readers see the actual content.

| Title example | Why Low |
|---|---|
| A title implying a movie sequel is definitely coming soon, when the page is only rumor | Somewhat misleading: suggests a confirmed release date |
| A title about the world ending and panic over a "sea monster", for an article about a fish carcass washed up on the shore | Exaggerated and shocking, mismatched with content |
| A breathless title about "the world's healthiest foods" to extend your life, for tips on adding fruit and veg to meals | Overblown compared to basic content |

**Leniency for ordinary people's creations:** on social media or sharing sites, many people post activities for friends (e.g., a first attempt at a new hobby). Don't rate Low for lack of talent unless that lack makes the page fail its purpose.

### 3.1 "Repackaged" content: low effort, originality and added value (5.2.1, p. 61)

- Some pages "repackage" content from higher-quality sites.
- **Lowest vs Low boundary:**
  - **Lowest:** all/nearly all MC copied/paraphrased/embedded/reposted with **little or no** effort, originality or added value.
  - **Low:** **much** of the MC comes from elsewhere, with **limited** effort to add value through editing, manual curation, reformatting or inserting original content.
- Low examples: social reposts with little commentary; pages of embedded videos or repinned images with little commentary/curation; "best" lists stitched together from reviews and rankings that already exist, adding little of their own.
- Applies to **any technique**, compared with other pages on similar content.

### 3.2 Filler as a poor experience (5.2.2, p. 62)

- The most helpful MC should sit **at the top and most prominently**. Example: on a recipe page, the recipe and directly related info should be near the top.
- Supporting content is fine if placed lower and less prominently.
- **Filler** = low-effort content occupying valuable, prominent space without serving the main purpose. It inflates a page so it looks rich when it isn't.
- **Low if:** (1) lots of low-quality filler creates a bad experience, or (2) lots of visually prominent filler makes the useful MC hard to find.

## 4. Low due to distracting Ads or SC (5.3, p. 62)

- Ads and SC are expected to be visible. But if ads, SC or interstitials **seriously hinder** access to the MC → usually Low, even if some MC is useful.
- **Not very distracting:** a single pop-over or interstitial with a clear, easy close button.
- **Very distracting:** hard-to-close ads that follow scrolling; interstitials requiring an app download.
- Ad/SC **content** can distract too: sexually suggestive images, gross images, sexual ads on non-sexual pages, or shocking/exaggerated titles and images.

## 5. Low due to reputation or site info (5.4–5.6, pp. 62–63)

**Mildly negative reputation (5.4):**
- Low if the site or creator has a mildly negative reputation (not malicious or financial fraud; that's Lowest).
- Well-known creators (journalists, authors, influencers, bloggers, vloggers, professionals such as lawyers/doctors) should have reputation info. For ordinary people and small creators, no info is normal and **not** a Low signal.
- For businesses: gather enough reviews and read negative ones in detail before concluding. Some service complaints are normal for stores and restaurants.

**Unsatisfying site/creator info (5.5):**
- Most sites are expected to make clear who created the MC unless there's a legitimate reason for anonymity. A long-used alias can serve as identity.
- For personal sites or non-YMYL forums, an email address or social link alone may suffice.
- **Stores and financial-transaction sites** need high trust. With only an email and a physical address, getting help with a problem is hard.
- **Low if:** pages with payment/transaction functions have unsatisfying customer service or contact info; YMYL or high-trust pages have unsatisfying info on who's responsible/who created the content.

**Exaggerated or mildly misleading site/creator info (5.6):**
- **Deceptive** info → Lowest. **Exaggerated or mildly misleading** info (e.g., experience/expertise claims that seem inflated to impress) → **Low**.
- E-E-A-T should rest on the MC, reputation research and verifiable credentials, not a simple "I'm an expert" claim.

## 6. Low examples (5.7, pp. 64–67)

- **News article on nuclear power (YMYL):** mostly common knowledge, poor writing (little effort), MC broken up by large disruptive ads → Low.
- **"Detox after the holidays" article:** misleading title (doesn't explain how to detox or what toxins mean); poor writing with meaningless statements; no sources; no evidence of expertise → Low.
- **40th birthday party food ideas:** lots of meaningless filler, no editing, only common info with no expertise or first-hand experience → Low or Lowest+.
- **Paraphrased science article with errors:** many grammar and punctuation errors; seems paraphrased from another science article with factual errors introduced → Lowest+ to Low.
- **Office dress tips:** unsatisfying MC, inaccurate, low E-E-A-T → Low.
- **Ginger health benefits:** garbled or meaningless content, no editing, unrelated links mid-page, medical claims with no expertise or sources → Low or Lowest+.
- **How to adopt a child from a particular country (YMYL):** steps are only common knowledge (e.g., "choose an agency" and look in a phone book), almost nothing country-specific, author bio shows no adoption expertise; some ads formatted like article links are hard to tell apart → Low.
- **Summary of a discussion thread:** little original content, low effort, little value over the source → Low.
- **Stock-picking advice (financial YMYL):** shallow, generic advice like checking key stats or finding firms with rising revenue; no evidence of financial expertise → Low.
- **How long the flu lasts (medical YMYL):** no evidence of medical expertise for advice-giving content → Low.
- **Apple pie recipe:** doesn't explain how to make the crust or list a ready-made crust as an ingredient, so it's nearly impossible to follow; no user reviews/comments to judge it → Low.
- **Gluten-free cheesecake recipe:** large ads in the main column push the MC down; the right column holds "Top Posts & Pages" that may be ads or SC, with shocking, racy, unrelated images → Low.
- **Recipe for a drink from a fictional world at a theme park:** ingredients are up top, but the actual recipe appears only after long scrolling; the "jump to recipe" link is hard to find; in between are photos and reviews of unrelated foods plus ads, SC and interstitials → Low. **Could rate higher** if better organized, with the recipe prominent and less filler/ads.

## 7. Medium: definition, criteria, two types (6.0–6.1, pp. 68–69)

**Medium** = beneficial purpose that **is achieved**. Nothing is wrong with Medium, and Medium pages are very common.

| Aspect | Medium criteria |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Beneficial or harmless |
| Potential harm | Not expected to cause harm |
| Topic/site type | Any; extra scrutiny for YMYL and online stores |
| Title | Summarizes the page |
| Ads and SC | Don't block or seriously interfere with the MC (ads alone aren't a consideration) |
| Site/creator info | Adequate for the purpose; for stores/transactions, check customer service; an alias is enough for personal forum/social content |
| MC quality | Adequate effort, originality, talent or skill so the purpose is met |
| Reputation | Neither especially positive nor especially negative |
| E-E-A-T | Adequate for the purpose |

**Two types of Medium:**
1. **Nothing wrong, but nothing special.**
2. **Mixed, with redeeming qualities:** some High signals (E-E-A-T, MC, positive reputation) alongside one Low signal or a few mild Low signals.

To rate above Medium, a page must meet High/Highest criteria. To rate below, it must meet Low/Lowest criteria.

## 8. Medium examples (6.2, pp. 69–70)

All achieve their purpose but show no High traits:
- **Short encyclopedia article on baroque pearls** (narrow topic): no further interesting, meaningful content.
- **Page from the humor site of objects with drawn-on mouths:** no discussion, engagement or comments from other users.
- **Articles on a reality show and on a singer** on an entertainment news site: no interesting content or original images.
- **Casserole recipe on a newspaper site** from a cookbook author: no related SC like reviews or links to other recipes, no original photos or comments from people who tried it.
- **Kids' cake recipe on a site known for quality animal and environment content:** little MC and SC, no more detailed steps or inspiration.

**Pattern:** what separates Medium from High is usually **visible effort**: interesting content, original images, user engagement (reviews, comments), detailed steps.

## 9. Implications for content creators

**[Application]**
- **Put the answer at the top.** For informational articles and recipes, the core answer/recipe goes first; background and story below. Provide an easy-to-find "jump to".
- **Cut filler.** Long intros full of common facts and definitions everyone knows are filler.
- **Titles must be proportionate to content.** Avoid superlatives and sensational claims the article doesn't deliver.
- **"Best" articles/product listicles:** without testing or first-hand experience, Low at best. Add testing, your own photos, data and selection criteria.
- **YMYL topics:** without evidence of author expertise (or an expert reviewer), health/finance advice tends toward Low even if well written.
- **Ads:** avoid ads in the main column that push the MC down, ads that look like article links, and racy or shocking ad images.
- **Expertise claims must be verifiable.** Inflated claims drop the rating to Low.
- **Medium isn't bad**, but to compete in competitive SERPs, aim for High: visible effort, originality and strong E-E-A-T (see `06-pq-high-highest.md`).
