## 1. High: baseline requirements and criteria (7.0, p. 71)

**High** = beneficial purpose **achieved well**. Possible for any purpose: information, selling, humor, artistic expression.

**Baseline (must be met):** beneficial purpose; not expected to cause harm; title summarizes the content; ads/SC don't block or seriously interfere with the MC; adequate site/creator info (for stores/transactions, check customer service; an alias is enough for personal forum/social content). Extra scrutiny for YMYL and online stores.

**Then at least one of:**
- MC showing **a lot of effort, originality, talent or skill** so the purpose is achieved well.
- **Positive reputation** of the site (for the page topic) or creator (for the MC topic).
- **High E-E-A-T** for the page's purpose.

## 2. High vs low quality MC (7.1, p. 72)

- High MC must be satisfying and show effort, originality, talent or skill. Informational pages: accurate and consistent with expert consensus where it exists.
- **Keep standards high.** Calibrate by comparing other pages on the same topic: "typical" and "average" pages are generally **Medium**, not High.

| Dimension | High signals | Low signals |
|---|---|---|
| Effort | Organized, edited and curated to serve the purpose. E.g., accurate, deep, clear news; a Q&A/forum thread with meaningful discussion from many people; a tidy craft tutorial with clear instructions | Useful content mixed with distracting filler; a forum post with little discussion or shallow comments; a tutorial with filler on top and a token explanation below |
| Originality | MC unique to the site; original photos/video; personal perspective from first-hand experience | Summaries of other sources with little added value; photos/video from elsewhere; summaries of other people's views (e.g., summarizing others' product reviews) |
| Talent/skill | Showcases the creator's talent (e.g., a talented dancer's video); purpose met thanks to skill (e.g., a plumbing guide by a skilled plumber) | Lacking skill so it fails (e.g., a plumbing guide by someone who can't explain the steps correctly) |

## 3. Positive reputation and high E-E-A-T (7.2–7.3, pp. 72–73)

**Positive reputation:**
- Site reputation counts if the site is responsible for the MC. If not (social/forum posts), the creator's reputation counts.
- For non-YMYL, evidence can be informal: popularity, engagement, user reviews. A site popular and well known for its topic/content type can count as positively reputed.
- Many small sites and ordinary people have no reputation info. **A page can still be High without it.**

**High E-E-A-T (trustworthy or very trustworthy pages):**
- **Experience** adds value on nearly every topic. Social posts and forum discussions are often High when they carry people's experience, from composing symphonies to reviewing home appliances.
- **Expertise** is needed for many topics, from hobbies (photography) to YMYL (tax preparation). Much informal expertise is visible in the MC itself.
- **Authoritativeness** exists everywhere: government tax sites for tax forms; local businesses/organizations for local info.
- **Trust** matters most for financial transactions and YMYL. Product reviews and advice pages also need at least some Trust. Trustworthy pages tend to be satisfying.

Key question: **what kind of E-E-A-T does this page need to achieve its purpose well?**

## 5. Highest: requirements and criteria (8.0–8.3, pp. 77–78)

**Highest** = beneficial purpose **achieved very well**. What separates it from High: MC quality, reputation and/or E-E-A-T.

**Baseline** as for High. **Then at least one of:**
- MC showing **exceptional** effort, originality, talent or skill so the purpose is met **very well**.
- **Very positive** reputation of site or creator for the topic.
- **Very high** E-E-A-T.

**Very high quality MC (8.1):** highly satisfying; among the most outstanding content on its topic. For informational pages: accurate, clearly communicated, consistent with expert consensus. Standards differ by type:
- **News:** original reporting that reveals what wasn't known; accurate, in-depth, investigative; describes primary sources and original reporting used; meets professional journalistic standards.
- **Artistic content** (video, images, photography, writing): unique, original work by highly skilled creators. If tied to a YMYL topic (e.g., meant to sway opinion on YMYL), YMYL standards apply.
- **Informational content:** original, accurate, comprehensive, clear, reflecting expert consensus. Expectations vary by type (scientific paper vs a hobby post about stamp collecting), but all must be accurate and clear.

**Very positive reputation (8.2):**
- Reputation research matters when giving Highest.
- YMYL: usually rests on endorsements from recognized experts or professional bodies, or prestigious awards.
- Non-YMYL: can be informal (popularity, engagement, reviews). Very positive if seen as one of the best online sources for the topic.
- Even without reputation info, a page **can still be Highest**.

**Very high E-E-A-T (8.3):** the key distinguisher for Highest. It can come from being the uniquely authoritative source for a topic; from deep experience (where experience is the main basis of trust); or from very high expertise. Such sources are the ones people trust most online for that topic.

Guiding questions: how important is first-hand experience here? Who counts as an expert? What gives a source strong authority? What makes a site/creator trustworthy for this topic?

## 6. Highest examples (8.4, pp. 79–84)

**News and journalism**
- Feature article on environmental pollution (YMYL) in a newspaper with many awards: in-depth reporting by experienced investigative journalists.
- Investigative article in an award-winning newspaper; its two reporters were finalists for a prestigious journalism award for that work.
- Fact-check page on a well-known debunking site verifying a photo that claims a mermaid discovery: original, skilled fact-checking, strong reputation and expertise for this kind of story.

**Information and reference**
- Ball-gown wedding dress page on a very popular wedding-planning site: lots of photos, price and style filters.
- National park information page on the park's official site, including traffic alerts: **uniquely authoritative**.
- Software tips on the software maker's official site: highly authoritative.
- Linux kernel source archive: the most authoritative source for its topic.

**Finance (YMYL)**
- The official US credit-report site, the sole source required and authorized by federal law.
- Tax forms and publications page on the federal tax agency site: uniquely authoritative and trustworthy.

**Magazines**
- In-depth interview with a musician in a magazine famous for music-artist content.
- In-depth, original article in a magazine with many national magazine awards.

**Medical (YMYL)**
- BMI calculator on a site of an institute that is part of the US national health institutes: functional, easy to use, made with effort and skill.
- Meningitis reference page on a nonprofit medical research group's site known as one of the best medical information sources.
- Seasonal flu reference page on a trustworthy, authoritative medical site.
- Health info on a hospital site rated among the best in the US.

**Shopping, login, charity**
- School backpack page on the maker's own site with full product info and hundreds of user reviews: the maker is the expert on its own product (authoritative) and known for one of the best backpacks.
- Login page of a large trusted bank, clearly showing what you're logging into.
- Homepage of a disaster-relief charity rated highly by several charity evaluators.

**Creative and other**
- A recipe blog documenting extensive experiments with a chocolate chip cookie recipe: **experience** plus expertise visible in a large amount of original, very satisfying content.
- PDF campus map of a major university on its official site: complete with buildings, parking and construction areas.
- A band's professional music video on its official channel: original and highly skilled.
- A viral amateur video by a film student about his mother's cat, winner of a cat-video festival award: original, high effort. **Amateurs can reach Highest.**
- An episode of a TV comedy show on the network's official site: the go-to source for that content.

## 7. Implications for content creators

**[Application]**
- **For most blog articles the realistic target is High.** Highest usually goes to uniquely official/authoritative sources, very highly reputed institutions, or truly outstanding content.
- **"Average" = Medium.** To reach High, an article must beat typical top results: more accurate, better organized, more original, or clearly written by someone experienced/expert.
- **Provable sources of effort:** your own photos and video, testing, primary data, interviews, case studies, documented experiments (like the cookie recipe blog).
- **Official sites always win for their own topic.** For official info (government requirements, fees, schedules), third-party content should cite the official source and add value through explanation, step-by-step guidance or experience.
- **Online stores:** unique products, complete info, many user reviews and detailed customer service are the recipe for High-to-Highest product pages.
- **Features are MC too.** A well-working calculator, filter, map or tool can lift the rating.
- **Content in accordions/tabs still counts as MC**, so it's fine to use them to tidy long pages on mobile.
